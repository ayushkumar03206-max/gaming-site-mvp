# Reference review (inspiration only)

On 2026-11-17 the two reference pages were opened in a browser and reviewed only at a high level.
**No code, artwork, logos, text or assets were copied.**

## Website reference: what we observed

- Light theme. Header with logo, central search and a simple nav. Hero carousel. Grid of category tiles. Horizontal rows of game cards (Popular, New, Featured). Blog teasers. Newsletter box.
- Game cards show a thumbnail, title, category and a play-count number.
- The game page has a breadcrumb, a large player with a blurred poster and a Play button, an action bar (like, favorite, share, fullscreen), a metadata panel, screenshots and a long description.

## How Gridlight Arcade differs

- Dark neon visual identity with its own logo mark, palette and copy.
- **No play counts, likes or ratings.** We have no verified data, so the "Trending" section is labelled as curated and "Popular" only reflects the visitor's own play history on their device.
- The iframe loads only when the visitor presses Play.
- The licensing page is generated from rights records.

## Game reference: Wrong Way

Observed genre: a Quoridor-style race. Each turn a player moves a pawn or places a two-square **wall
between cells**. There are 2- or 4-player modes, AI tiers, levels and powers.

**Pathforge** shares only the broad genre (a race-and-obstruct board game with pathfinding). Its rules
and presentation are original:

- Blocks fill whole **cells**, not edges, and there are no walls or wall trays.
- A smaller default board (7×7) with **spark cells** that award extra blocks.
- A straight **vault** over an adjacent rival, with no diagonal side-steps.
- Its own AI tiers, visuals and text.
- No levels, powers, coins, themes or chests.
